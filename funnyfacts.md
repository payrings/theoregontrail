# Your Broken Arm Is Fine: 30 Oregon Trail Curiosities

By Giacomo Paganelli - rift-demote-fence@duck.com

## Myths and design
1. **Nobody dies of dysentery in this game.** The famous line "You have died of dysentery" does not exist in the game. The game says "[name] has dysentery" in one place and "[name] has died" in another, and it never puts the two together. As far as the game is concerned, your party members die of mysterious causes, and the dysentery is just a coincidence.
2. **There's no separate formula for dying.** If someone catches a second disease before they've recovered from the first, they die, and the second disease is never mentioned. The designer chose this to keep the game simple, and it means poor health costs you more lives. Nobody is told which disease finished them off.
3. **The river rules come from the size of a real wagon.** A wagon has about 30 inches of clearance, which is why you can only ford a river up to 2.5 feet deep. The Indian guide first cut the risks by only 50%, but testers kept having accidents anyway and decided he wasn't worth his fee. The developers made him much more reliable, cutting the risks by 80%.
4. **There's a secret menu for teachers.** Press Control-A at the title menu and you get a management screen where a teacher can reset the top ten (option 3) and erase embarrassing gravestones written by naughty kids (option 4). Nothing on the menu tells you it's there, and you can't remove a single name, only the whole list.
5. **You can shoot a bison, but you can't carry it home.** A bison weighs 1,700 to 2,000 pounds, but the game lets you bring back at most 100 pounds per hunt. The designer wanted to keep the wagon's load believable and make players think twice about wasting meat.
6. **The game only remembers two gravestones.** Ten would have interrupted play too often, so there's one grave per disk side, and a new one replaces the old.
7. **Children got good at hunting fast.** Adults found the hunting game too hard, and the designer kept it that way on purpose. In testing, many children became keen hunters after only three or four tries.

## Odd corners and bugs
8. **A broken arm does nothing.** The game numbers injuries and illnesses, and the broken arm is number 0, which is also the number for "healthy". So the game thinks the person is fine even with a broken arm!
9. **Thieves never take your money.** The code does have a branch for stealing cash, but the thing a thief can pick is never the cash item, so it never happens. And if a thief finds nothing to take, the game shows no message at all.
10. **February always has 28 days.** That's true even in 1848, which was a leap year.
11. **If you have exactly the Barlow Road toll, you can't pay it.** The game checks that you have more than the toll, so a party with exactly enough money for the toll is told nothing and sent back to choose again. A single cent more and the road opens. Why?
12. **The final screen gets the century wrong after 1899.** It prints the year as "18" followed by a stored number, so 1905 appears as 18105.
13. **Waiting at a river is completely safe.** Nobody falls ill or dies while the party waits, however long it takes. A researcher once made a party wait 14,272 game years, which is a very patient wagon. When it finally crossed, it was in such bad shape that it died within days.
14. **The game crashes if the year goes past 2055.** The game stores the year in a single byte, which overflows, and the game stops with "Error 53 at line #50050".
15. **The game has climate data for Portland, but it's never used.** The code can only reach five climate zones, numbered 0 to 4, so the sixth row, for Portland, is never read. Sorry Portland! The designer's book describes six zones; the shipped game has five.
16. **The first disk side is completely full, but the second has a leftover.** Side 1 has no free space at all. Side 2, however, still holds a developer's test program for the rafting game, which the game never runs.
17. **The game remembers which disease you have, whatever the book says.** The designer's book says the program doesn't keep track of which disease a person has. It does: the number is stored for the whole illness. It is just never shown to you, so it feels as if the book were right.
18. **You flip the disk much earlier than "about halfway".** The book says halfway, but the flip comes at Fort Laramie, 640 miles in, which is only about a third (34%) of the 1,871-mile route by the Barlow Road.

## Numbers, numbers, numbers
19. **Your chance of falling ill each day is between 1% and 10.3%.** This depends on the party's health: 1% when everyone is perfectly well and 10.3% at the worst health the game allows. The lead designer's book states the range was 0% to 40%, but the code does something else. Luckily for the party, it's gentler than the book.
20. **Several things can happen on the same day.** Each day, the game checks fifteen possible events one after another, and it doesn't stop at the first one that happens. Yes, it can rain, break a wheel and bring a fever on the same Tuesday. The designer's book says the opposite: no more than one random event a day. The code never enforced that.
21. **The trail is kinder than the book says.** For three groups of events the book lists 3% (lose the trail or take a wrong one), 4% (a fire, a lost member or a stray ox) and 4% (an abandoned wagon or a thief), 11% a day in all. The code gives 2%, 1% and 2%: 5% a day, about half as much.
22. **Wagon breakdowns are missing from the book's list.** The book lists an injured ox at 2% and an injured person at 2% on the prairie (3.5% each in the mountains). In the code these are one event of 4% (7% in the mountains), and a third of it is a broken wagon wheel, axle or tongue. The rest is split between the ox and the person, so each is only 1.34%.
23. **Some weather details are not what the book says.** Snake bites are harmless unless the weather is warm or hotter. Fog starts at the Snake River crossing, one landmark later than "after Fort Hall". And a heavy snow day drops 6.4 inches, not the book's 8.
24. **The mountains slow you by 40%, not 50%.** The book says half speed in the mountains. The code goes from 20 miles a day to 12, which is 0.6, and it does that at Fort Laramie, well before the mountain trouble (rough and impassable trails) starts at Fort Hall.
25. **The weather table is written in inches of rain.** Each rain value in the table is 30 plus ten times the month's average rainfall in inches, so Kansas City's 4.8 inches of rain in June is stored as 78. But the game rolls the dice every day, and a simulated month gives only about a third (0.34) of that rain by my calculation.
26. **Nobody can score less than 250.** A lone banker in very poor health, with nothing left but the wagon itself, scores exactly 250. That is Elijah White's score at the bottom of the original top ten, and a tie doesn't count, so this lowest party doesn't make the list. Add one ox and it's 254, and you do.
27. **The top-ten list has a typo in the book.** The seventh name in the game is Mary Bartlett; the designer's book calls her "Barlett".
28. **A hunter is as likely to meet a rabbit as a bison.** Each new animal is drawn evenly from the kinds allowed at that point on the trail. The book wondered whether the frequency weighting, with common animals more likely, ever made it into the game. It didn't. Of the six animals, the code names none; by weight and region four are clearly a bison, deer, rabbit and squirrel, and the book's text suggests the other two are a pronghorn and, less surely, a bear.
29. **A ford can ruin more than the book says.** If you cross a deep river and lose goods, each item is lost with a chance of depth divided by ten, and then you lose anywhere from one unit to all of it. The average loss matches the book's estimate, but the worst case is far worse than "up to 40%".
30. **The ferry makes you wait at least two days.** The book says one to six days. The code says two to six.
