# Dialogue records

The 51 monologues the game offers at its seventeen landmarks, three per landmark, each a speaker and a text. This is the replacement dialogue written for this project; it is not the original game's text. The Python and the TypeScript rebuilds each ship an identical copy of this file. The format is the one the games read: a header line `Landmark N (name), slot S`, then `Speaker:` and `Text:`. A text is at most 240 characters, so that the game's 240-character read never has to be continued.

### Landmark 0 (Independence)

```
Landmark 0 (Independence), slot 0
  Speaker: A wheelwright named Ezra Dunmore
  Text: Every wagon looks sound in the yard. The prairie finds the weak spot by the second week. Know your wheels, your axles and your tongue before you leave, and carry a spare of each if your purse will stretch.

Landmark 0 (Independence), slot 1
  Speaker: A grocer's wife, Hattie Moss
  Text: Flour, bacon and beans keep best. Buy more food than you think you will eat, for the road is long and the shops are few. A hungry family will pay any price for a sack of meal.

Landmark 0 (Independence), slot 2
  Speaker: A boatman from the Missouri
  Text: Leave too early and there is no grass for your oxen. Leave too late and the mountains meet you with snow. Half of this journey is simply choosing the right month to start.
```

### Landmark 1 (the Kansas River crossing)

```
Landmark 1 (the Kansas River crossing), slot 0
  Speaker: A ferryman's son
  Text: Pa charges by the wagon, and today the line is long. Folks who wait their turn cross dry. Folks in a hurry try the ford and lose a wheel, or worse. The river is wider than it looks from the bank.

Landmark 1 (the Kansas River crossing), slot 1
  Speaker: A farmer from the Kansa nation
  Text: Our fields lie along this river. Wagons pass every day now, and the game keeps moving away from the road. Buy fresh meat when you can. Do not count on finding any later.

Landmark 1 (the Kansas River crossing), slot 2
  Speaker: A returning teamster named Silas Pike
  Text: Let your team graze on the green grass while it lasts. Oxen that start the road tired end it lying down. Watch how they walk each morning, and slow down before they ask you to.
```

### Landmark 2 (the Big Blue River crossing)

```
Landmark 2 (the Big Blue River crossing), slot 0
  Speaker: A scout riding east
  Text: The Big Blue rises fast after a rain. If the water is up to the wagon bed, do not force it. One day of waiting costs less than a drowned team and a spoiled load.

Landmark 2 (the Big Blue River crossing), slot 1
  Speaker: A mother of five, Louisa Garrity
  Text: We camped three days on the bank waiting for the water to fall. The damp gave the children coughs. Keep everyone warm and dry. Sickness travels faster than any wagon.

Landmark 2 (the Big Blue River crossing), slot 2
  Speaker: A teamster named Cyrus Bell
  Text: Go slowly into the ford and keep the team moving once they are wet. If you stop in the middle, the wheels sink. Walk beside the lead ox and talk to him quietly.
```

### Landmark 3 (Fort Kearney)

```
Landmark 3 (Fort Kearney), slot 0
  Speaker: The post surgeon, Dr. Amos Fenn
  Text: Most of the sickness I treat comes from bad water and tired bodies. Drink from running streams and avoid still pools. A day's rest does more good than anything in my medicine chest.

Landmark 3 (Fort Kearney), slot 1
  Speaker: A buffalo hunter named Gideon Roark
  Text: The herds cross the Platte about now. One good shot gives more meat than a wagon can carry, so take only what you can haul. Steady your aim first. A wasted bullet is a wasted meal.

Landmark 3 (Fort Kearney), slot 2
  Speaker: A sergeant of the garrison
  Text: This is the last real post before the high plains. Mail goes east from here once a week. Write home, buy what you lack, and do not expect another store for a long while.
```

### Landmark 4 (Chimney Rock)

```
Landmark 4 (Chimney Rock), slot 0
  Speaker: A young surveyor, Percy Lund
  Text: You can see the Rock two days before you reach it. The needle stands some three hundred feet over the plain. I have sketched it six times and the folks at home will still not believe me.

Landmark 4 (Chimney Rock), slot 1
  Speaker: A widow named Margaret Ashby
  Text: My Thomas lies in a grave back on the plains, and I mean to see his children to Oregon. Eat sensibly every day. A family that feasts early on the trail goes hungry late.

Landmark 4 (Chimney Rock), slot 2
  Speaker: An old trapper called Mose
  Text: The wind comes down off the bluffs hard, and the nights turn cold quickly. An extra set of clothes for each person costs less than a funeral. Take my word on that.
```

### Landmark 5 (Fort Laramie)

```
Landmark 5 (Fort Laramie), slot 0
  Speaker: A Lakota trader
  Text: My people have traded at this fort for many years. Blankets, kettles and good knives are worth more to us than coin. Bring something useful and we will deal fairly with you.

Landmark 5 (Fort Laramie), slot 1
  Speaker: The fort blacksmith
  Text: Wagons come in here with tires loose and axles cracked from the dry air. I can mend most things, but I cannot make an ox. Go over your wagon carefully before the mountains.

Landmark 5 (Fort Laramie), slot 2
  Speaker: A woman from Ohio, Prudence Hale
  Text: The road ahead is long and the company thins out. Share what you know with the other families you meet. The party that helps its neighbors is the party that arrives.
```

### Landmark 6 (Independence Rock)

```
Landmark 6 (Independence Rock), slot 0
  Speaker: A boy named Tobias
  Text: I scratched my name on the rock with a nail! Pa says we must reach it by the Fourth of July or the snow will catch us in the mountains. So far we are on time.

Landmark 6 (Independence Rock), slot 1
  Speaker: A traveling preacher
  Text: Countless names are carved into this granite, and not every carver reached Oregon. Be careful of the road, friend. The rock has a long memory.

Landmark 6 (Independence Rock), slot 2
  Speaker: A freighter named Orrin Tate
  Text: Past here the grass grows thin and some of the water turns foul. Fill your barrels at every good spring you pass, and let the animals drink well before you move on.
```

### Landmark 7 (South Pass)

```
Landmark 7 (South Pass), slot 0
  Speaker: A packer from Salt Lake
  Text: Do not look for a gate. The Pass is wide and gentle, and you will cross the Divide without noticing it. Every drop of rain that falls on the far side runs toward the Pacific.

Landmark 7 (South Pass), slot 1
  Speaker: A fur trader named Lucien Barbeau
  Text: Warm by day and freezing by night. One cold snap in these hills, and a family without coats is in trouble. Keep your blankets close and your firewood dry.

Landmark 7 (South Pass), slot 2
  Speaker: A girl named Josephine
  Text: Mama says we have walked a thousand miles. I counted my steps for the first ten and gave up. Look, you can see the white tops of the Wind River mountains from here!
```

### Landmark 8 (Fort Bridger)

```
Landmark 8 (Fort Bridger), slot 0
  Speaker: The post trader
  Text: Our prices are high and we make no secret of it. Everything here was hauled a long way. Still, a good yoke of oxen is a fair bargain at any price once yours have gone lame.

Landmark 8 (Fort Bridger), slot 1
  Speaker: A woman headed for California
  Text: The papers praise a shorter road south, written by men who never took it. Look hard at any route that promises to save you days. A long road you know is better than a short one you guess at.

Landmark 8 (Fort Bridger), slot 2
  Speaker: A teamster named Nate Cobb
  Text: Hunt when you can, friend. Food at the forts costs a fortune, and the game is better up in the meadows ahead than it was back on the plain.
```

### Landmark 9 (the Green River crossing)

```
Landmark 9 (the Green River crossing), slot 0
  Speaker: The ferry master, Lemuel Ward
  Text: The Green is wide, cold and quick. By afternoon the snow melt has lifted it well above its morning mark. I will take you over for a fee, and by evening you will be glad you paid it.

Landmark 9 (the Green River crossing), slot 1
  Speaker: A trapper named Baptiste
  Text: Beaver used to gather here by the thousand. Now it is wagons that gather. Keep your cattle out of the soft mud along the bank. It holds a hoof tighter than you would believe.

Landmark 9 (the Green River crossing), slot 2
  Speaker: A girl named Ada
  Text: Our dog swam the whole river and then shook himself all over the wagon! Papa laughed so hard he cried. It was the first time he laughed since we left Fort Laramie.
```

### Landmark 10 (Soda Springs)

```
Landmark 10 (Soda Springs), slot 0
  Speaker: An emigrant named Wendell Ames
  Text: The water bubbles up out of the ground, sharp and fizzy like lemonade. A cup or two is a treat. Do not drink it all day, though, for it sits heavily on the stomach.

Landmark 10 (Soda Springs), slot 1
  Speaker: A woman with a basket, Tabitha Quill
  Text: There are old lava beds close by, black and rough. They will cut an ox's hooves to ribbons. Keep to the wagon track and take your time.

Landmark 10 (Soda Springs), slot 2
  Speaker: A boy named Elias
  Text: I climbed the ridge and found a nest in the rock! Mama says we cannot stop. The next place is Fort Hall, and after that comes the Snake River.
```

### Landmark 11 (Fort Hall)

```
Landmark 11 (Fort Hall), slot 0
  Speaker: A clerk at the trading post
  Text: Many parties decide here whether to go on to Oregon or turn for California. Settle it plainly among yourselves. Quarrels on the trail cost more miles than any detour.

Landmark 11 (Fort Hall), slot 1
  Speaker: A wagon master named Dolan
  Text: From here the Snake River country is dry and rough. Check your supplies tonight, for the next store is far off. Buy food now, and spare parts if you can pay for them.

Landmark 11 (Fort Hall), slot 2
  Speaker: A traveler named Clarice Webb
  Text: Forty wagons passed this week. Some had no spare tongue, some had no oxen to spare. It is easy to be careless when the ground is flat. The mountains are not flat.
```

### Landmark 12 (the Snake River crossing)

```
Landmark 12 (the Snake River crossing), slot 0
  Speaker: A worried father
  Text: We watched a family try the ford last week. They lost two oxen and half their flour. The Snake does not forgive guessing. If the river looks deep, it is.

Landmark 12 (the Snake River crossing), slot 1
  Speaker: A mule skinner named Garrett Ives
  Text: If you choose to float, seal the wagon box tight. A ferry costs money, but money spends more easily than lives. Never cross with a loose load.

Landmark 12 (the Snake River crossing), slot 2
  Speaker: A young bride, Eleanor Voss
  Text: They say the falls upstream are the loveliest sight on the whole road. I will not see them. We must be across before the next storm comes down the valley.
```

### Landmark 13 (Fort Boise)

```
Landmark 13 (Fort Boise), slot 0
  Speaker: A clerk of the Hudson's Bay Company
  Text: We trade in salmon, flour and furs. Prices are fair, and the salmon is fresh when the run is good. The next supplies are a long haul from here, so buy what you need.

Landmark 13 (Fort Boise), slot 1
  Speaker: A woman with a lame ox, Rosalind Tuck
  Text: My Duke went lame two days ago on the sagebrush plain. Look at your animals' feet every night. A limping ox slows the whole wagon, and a slow wagon runs out of food.

Landmark 13 (Fort Boise), slot 2
  Speaker: A farmer named Hugh Mallory
  Text: They say Oregon is all rain and green, with soil that grows anything. I have come to believe it. Several hundred miles remain by my count. Keep your courage up.
```

### Landmark 14 (the Blue Mountains)

```
Landmark 14 (the Blue Mountains), slot 0
  Speaker: A guide named Reuben Shaw
  Text: The forest is thick here and the slopes are steep. Cut a path where you must and lock your wheels going downhill. A runaway wagon cannot be called back.

Landmark 14 (the Blue Mountains), slot 1
  Speaker: A mother in a woolen shawl
  Text: The snow comes early on the heights. We left Missouri in spring, and I never thought I would be cold again by August. Keep warm, keep moving, and keep the fire fed.

Landmark 14 (the Blue Mountains), slot 2
  Speaker: A boy named Peter
  Text: Pa let me carry the rifle this morning. I saw a deer in the trees, big as a horse! Maybe we will eat well tonight if I can hold still long enough.
```

### Landmark 15 (Fort Walla Walla)

```
Landmark 15 (Fort Walla Walla), slot 0
  Speaker: A mission teacher
  Text: Settlers are arriving on the Columbia every week, and many come with empty purses and no plan. Buy your flour here while you can. The river is not the end of the trail but the hardest part of it.

Landmark 15 (Fort Walla Walla), slot 1
  Speaker: A Cayuse horse trader
  Text: My people have raised horses on these plains for generations. If you need a fresh animal, trade honestly and we will do the same.

Landmark 15 (Fort Walla Walla), slot 2
  Speaker: A settler named Julian Brandt
  Text: The river is quick but dangerous, and the Barlow Road is long but dry. Think hard before you choose. You will be tired and short of supplies by then, and a rested party chooses better.
```

### Landmark 16 (The Dalles)

```
Landmark 16 (The Dalles), slot 0
  Speaker: A raftwright
  Text: A good raft is a set of logs lashed tight, with a steering oar at each end. The Columbia runs hard through the rapids. Keep the bow straight and stay well clear of the rocks.

Landmark 16 (The Dalles), slot 1
  Speaker: A toll keeper on the new road
  Text: The road through the mountains charges a toll for each wagon and each head of stock. It is rough, but it keeps you off the river. Count your money before you decide.

Landmark 16 (The Dalles), slot 2
  Speaker: A tired traveler named Beatrice Lang
  Text: The end of the journey is only weeks away. I thought I would feel brave. I only feel tired. But rain on green hills will look like heaven when you finally arrive.
```
